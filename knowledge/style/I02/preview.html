Kyoto Craft Institute · Research Report 2024

Ishikawa Prefecture Lacquerware Study · Series IV

Traditional Urushi Lacquerware Producers in Ishikawa Who Adopted Direct-to-Consumer Digital Channels Between 2018 and 2023 Grew Revenue by 64 Percent While Preserving Apprenticeship Lineages Intact.

A five-year longitudinal study of 112 urushi producers in Wajima and Yamanaka finds that digital channel adoption correlated strongly with revenue growth and apprentice retention. Producers who maintained wholesale-only models saw median revenue decline by 18 percent over the same period.

Study Period 2018 – 2023
Producers Surveyed 112 Artisans
Prefecture Ishikawa (Wajima & Yamanaka)
Lead Researcher Kyoto Craft Institute
01
Evidence · Apprenticeship & Revenue Analysis

Artisans Who Maintained Three or More Apprentices Through the Direct-Channel Transition Period Generated 2.3 Times the Revenue of Equivalent Craft Producers Who Continued Relying Exclusively on Wholesale Distribution.

2.3× Revenue Multiplier DTC adopters with 3+ apprentices vs. wholesale-only peers of matched scale
91% Apprentice Retention Rate among DTC adopters vs. 54% in wholesale-only cohort over study period
+¥2.4M Annual Revenue Gain Median incremental annual revenue per DTC artisan in years 3–5 of adoption

The revenue differential was most pronounced among producers who invested in both digital channel infrastructure and in-studio apprentice training simultaneously. Artisans who sequenced these investments — building the digital presence first, then expanding apprenticeships — showed a lag of approximately 14 months before revenue gains materialized. The cohort maintaining three or more apprentices throughout demonstrated the highest craft continuity scores and lowest production interruption rates, suggesting that apprentice depth functions as both an operational buffer and a brand signal to high-value direct customers.

78% of high-revenue DTC adopters

reported that direct customer relationships — established through digital channels — created recurring commissions that accounted for over 40% of annual revenue within two years of platform launch, displacing the need for trade fair dependence.
