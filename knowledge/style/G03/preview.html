Intergenerational Income Mobility in Six South Asian Megacities
Declined by 22 Percent Between 2000 and 2020, Reversing
Two Decades of Post-Liberalization Gains.

Drawing on longitudinal household panel data from Dhaka, Delhi, Karachi, Kolkata, Lahore, and Mumbai, this study estimates intergenerational income elasticities across three generational cohorts born in 1970, 1985, and 2000. Contrary to expectations formed during the 1990s liberalization era, upward mobility stalled markedly after 2005 and reversed across all six cities by 2020.

Coverage 6 megacities, 3 cohorts
Observation period 2000 – 2020
Household panel size 148,400 respondents
Methodology Rank-rank regression & IGE estimation

Rank-rank slope analysis reveals that the income rank of a father explains 43 percent more of the son's eventual rank in the 2000 birth cohort than in the 1985 cohort across both cities. The sharpest declines in mobility are concentrated in peri-urban zones with constrained secondary schooling access, suggesting that spatial inequality in educational infrastructure underlies the cohort divergence rather than aggregate labor market conditions alone.
