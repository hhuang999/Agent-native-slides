01 Nordic Centre for Educational Equity · Early Childhood Literacy

Structured Literacy Instruction in the First Three
Years of School Closes Two Thirds of the Reading
Gap Between Socioeconomic Groups by Age Nine.

A six-year longitudinal study of 14,800 children across 112 primary schools in Denmark, Finland, and Norway. Three structured literacy programmes compared against matched-curriculum standard instruction. Outcomes tracked through reading fluency, comprehension, and vocabulary assessments at ages six, seven, eight, and nine.

The structured literacy programmes shared three core components: explicit phonics instruction with systematic review, daily decodable text practice matched to current phonics knowledge, and teacher-directed vocabulary development within connected reading. The control group received the standard curriculum without these structured elements.

The gap-closure effect was not uniform across programmes. The two programmes with the highest lesson-structure fidelity scores — maintained through regular coaching visits — produced 78% gap closure by age nine. The third programme, implemented without ongoing teacher support after initial training, produced 44% closure. Fidelity to implementation matters as much as programme selection.
