Analysis of 312 events reveals that shared control-plane architectures are the primary propagation vector. When the management and data planes share physical network fabric, a localized failure in zone isolation can cascade as control-plane components attempt remediation and amplify the fault signal.

Hardware-isolated zone architectures — where control plane traffic traverses physically independent paths — show a cross-zone propagation rate of 11%, compared to 74% in shared-fabric designs. The difference is not primarily a software problem. It is an infrastructure topology decision made at the time of facility design.
