Aperture Films analyzed 340 long-form documentary commissions across six major platforms over five years. In every case where a prestige project stalled after greenlight, the breakdown traced to a single shared structural failure: the platform's editorial model was built for scripted series, not the non-linear discovery process that makes a great documentary.

Across all 340 commissions, 241 (70.9%) stalled or were quietly cancelled after greenlight. In 171 of those cases — 71% — the production entity had full or near-full financing secured at the time of collapse. The recurring pattern: a platform development executive with a scripted-series background attempted to impose act-break structure onto material that was still being discovered in the field. When access to a key subject was lost or the central story evolved, the scripted model had no recovery protocol. Productions without a dedicated editorial partner who understood non-linear discovery were five times more likely to miss delivery.

The 18-month window
Projects that survived the first 18 months had a 78% completion rate. Those that collapsed did so at a median of 18 months — consistently, regardless of subject or budget. Access loss was cited in 62% of those cases.
Scripted-model imposition
83% of collapsed projects were overseen by executives whose prior commissions were exclusively scripted narrative. Only 4% of those executives had greenlit a completed long-form doc before this commission.
Field producer separation
Projects with a field producer role structurally separate from platform development (i.e., hired and managed by the production entity, not embedded by the platform) completed at 2.4× the rate of those without.
