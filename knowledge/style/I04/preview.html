LearnPath Analytics · K–12 Adaptive Learning Outcomes Study Research Report 2025–26

Students Using Adaptive Learning Paths for 45 or More Minutes per Week Achieved Proficiency Milestones 2.7 Times Faster Than Peer Groups Following Standard Fixed-Curriculum Sequences Across All Seven Measured Subject Areas.

This study tracked 47,200 students across 312 schools in 18 districts over a full academic year, comparing adaptive and fixed-curriculum cohorts on standardized assessment gains and time-to-proficiency benchmarks.

Effect sizes were consistent across demographic subgroups and subject areas, with the strongest gains observed in mathematics (d = 0.82) and reading comprehension (d = 0.74).

Multilevel regression modeling with school-level random effects confirmed a robust dose-response relationship across all seven subjects. The 30-minute baseline was selected to represent the median usage in the control arm. Marginal returns decline above 75 minutes per week, suggesting an optimal range of 45–75 minutes for practical scheduling. Effects were homogeneous across grade bands (3–5, 6–8, 9–12), indicating the mechanism is not grade-specific.

Supplementary Finding

Reading Comprehension

d = 0.74

Second-highest effect size among all subjects. Vocabulary acquisition sub-scores showed disproportionate gains, particularly for English-language learners (d = 0.91 in that sub-group).

Sample details

n = 47,200 students; 18 districts; pre–post design with matched control cohorts; 36-week observation window; assessments: MAP Growth + district-designed benchmarks.
