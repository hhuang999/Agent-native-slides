Infrastructure debt in the 8–12% allocation band delivered a consistent illiquidity premium versus 10-year sovereign equivalents across all studied geographies, compensating investors for lock-up without materially increasing credit risk exposure.

Analysis across 62 mandates that held infrastructure debt within the 8–12% band shows a maximum drawdown of −2.1% versus −14.3% for the public market equivalent cohort during the same period. The stability derives from contracted long-duration cash flows, inflation-linked revenue structures, and the essential-service nature of the underlying assets — water, energy transmission, toll roads, and regulated utilities — which exhibit near-zero correlation to equity risk premia during risk-off periods.
