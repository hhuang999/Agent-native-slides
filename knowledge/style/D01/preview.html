Megaproject Cost Growth Is Not
Random. It Is Systematic,
Predictable, and Preventable.

The Lund Infrastructure Research Group analyzed 258 major public infrastructure projects completed between 2000 and 2025 in OECD member states. In every category — rail, road, bridge, hospital, airport — cost overrun followed a consistent structural pattern traceable to three procurement and governance failures that appear before construction begins.

The optimism bias literature has established that infrastructure cost overruns are widespread and non-random. This study adds a structural finding: the decisive events occur before the contract is awarded, not in the construction phase. In 217 of 258 projects, scope definitions at the procurement stage contained material gaps — missing utility relocations, unresolved land tenure, inadequate ground investigation, or undefined system integration — that were either known to project leadership or discoverable with standard review. The 11-year median suppression period between internal scope-gap identification and public cost revision reflects the political incentive to announce budget stability during election cycles, not genuine uncertainty about the numbers.

Rail, 74 projects
Median final cost 2.7× approved budget. Scope gaps in ground condition definition and station interface design were the leading categories, present in 81% of severe overruns.
Hospital, 48 projects
Median final cost 1.9× approved budget. Technology infrastructure scope gaps — medical gas, imaging, IT pathways — were present in 78% of severe overruns and consistently underspecified at procurement.
Airport, 31 projects
Highest absolute overruns; median 3.1× approved. Airside/landside interface definition and regulatory approval sequencing failures drove 88% of severe cases.
Road and bridge, 105 projects
Lowest median overrun at 1.6×. Better-established procurement templates for ground investigation and utility coordination in this category explain the relative performance.
