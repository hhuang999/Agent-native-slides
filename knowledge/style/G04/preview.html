Tropical Reforestation Zones Established Between 2015 and 2022 Sequester Carbon at 2.4 Times the Rate Predicted by Standard IPCC Tier 1 Models When Mycorrhizal Network Density Exceeds 85 Percent.

We report a systematic empirical assessment of net carbon sequestration across 140,000 ha of monitored tropical reforestation sites in Brazil, Indonesia, and the Democratic Republic of Congo spanning 2015–2022. Continuous eddy-covariance measurement paired with satellite-derived mycorrhizal network mapping reveals that mycorrhizal density is the primary modulating variable separating high- and low-performance sites, overriding rainfall, soil type, and stand age as predictors in multivariate regression.

Fixed-effects panel regression across all 312 monitored sites isolates mycorrhizal network density as an independent predictor of net sequestration after controlling for mean annual precipitation, soil organic carbon baseline, stand age, and canopy closure. The estimated marginal effect of 0.31 tCO₂/ha/yr per percentage point remains stable across all three regional subsamples (Brazil, Indonesia, DRC), suggesting a mechanism consistent with enhanced phosphorus cycling and below-ground carbon allocation rather than site-specific edaphic conditions. Compounded across the 140,000-hectare study area, the modelled departure from IPCC Tier 1 projections represents an economically material underestimate of tropical reforestation as a near-term mitigation lever under Paris Agreement accounting frameworks.

85%
MN density threshold
Sites exceeding 85% mycorrhizal network density show a discontinuous jump in sequestration efficiency. Below this threshold, gains are approximately linear; above it, network redundancy appears to buffer seasonal drought stress, sustaining carbon uptake through dry periods that would otherwise halt net flux at lower-density stands.
