Global Biodiversity Research Foundation  ·  Ecosystem Services Valuation Study

Intact Boreal Forest Zones in Northern Canada and Siberia Provide Measurable Ecosystem Services Valued at $3,200 per Hectare per Year When Carbon Sequestration, Water Regulation, and Biodiversity Habitat Functions Are Priced at Current Market Rates.

This study synthesizes field measurement data and market pricing frameworks across 6.2 million hectares of boreal forest to produce the first integrated per-hectare valuation spanning carbon, hydrological, and biodiversity dimensions. Findings establish baseline economic evidence for policy instruments targeting boreal conservation at national and international scales.

Field measurements across 312 stratified sites confirm a non-linear relationship between canopy continuity and ecosystem service delivery. Below 78% continuity, carbon sequestration rates plateau and hydrological regulation functions decline due to gap-driven turbulence effects. Above the threshold, every additional percentage point of intact canopy delivers a measurable and economically quantifiable increment in both carbon capture and watershed stability, with the strongest effects concentrated in zones experiencing seasonal freeze-thaw cycles.

At current market pricing, the 6.2-million-hectare study area generates an estimated $19.8 billion in annual ecosystem service value — a figure that exceeds the GDP of several small nations and underlines the macroeconomic stakes of boreal conservation policy.
