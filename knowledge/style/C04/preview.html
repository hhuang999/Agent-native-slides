Whisky Buyers Who Visit the Distillery
Spend Four Times More Than Those
Who Never Will.

Cairn & Stave Distilleries analyzed purchasing data from 28 Scottish single malt distilleries across five years, tracking lifetime spend, allocated-release conversion, and waitlist engagement. In every tier — from accessible expressions to limited cask releases — visitors exhibited materially higher loyalty and significantly greater tolerance for premium pricing than buyers acquired through retail or online channels alone.

02 / 04 — Provenance Economics

Visitors convert to allocated-release buyers at 4.2× the rate of non-visitors — and their median annual spend holds above £2,800 for at least five years after the first visit.

The provenance effect in luxury spirits has long been observed qualitatively — the idea that visiting a distillery deepens brand attachment — but this cohort provides the first cross-distillery quantification. Visitor conversion to allocated releases was 4.2× higher than non-visitor rates; this gap widened with the prestige of the release, reaching 6.1× for bottles priced above £500. The mechanism appears to be two-directional: visitors self-select for existing engagement, but the visit itself measurably increases spend in the following 12 months by a median of 38% versus the same customer's pre-visit baseline. Distilleries that added overnight immersive experiences since 2021 show the strongest effect — median annual spend from this cohort now accounts for 34% of total revenue, up from single digits five years ago.

Speyside cluster, 2020–2025
Seven Speyside distilleries in the study showed visitor-acquired customers with a 78-month median engagement window — nearly twice the 42-month window for non-visitors. The gap held across all price tiers.
Waitlist tolerance
Visitors accepted waitlists of 18+ months for allocated releases at 3.8× the rate of non-visitors. Among overnight-experience alumni this rose to 5.2×, with 91% of waitlist positions held to completion.
Price elasticity effect
Visitor-acquired buyers showed 22% lower price elasticity on annual increases versus non-visitors — absorbing premium adjustments at a point where non-visitors churned at 11%.
