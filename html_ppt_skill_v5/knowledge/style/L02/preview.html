Draft Model Sizing — Efficiency Tradeoffs

Each 100M-Parameter Reduction in Draft Model Size Below the Optimal 400M Threshold Costs 0.6× in Acceptance Rate and Recovers Only 0.4× in Wall-Clock Speedup — Making Under-Provisioning the Primary Source of Speculative Decoding Underperformance.

Systematic ablation across nine draft model sizes (100M–900M parameters) reveals a non-linear tradeoff surface: models below 400M parameters exhibit disproportionate acceptance-rate collapse, as the draft distribution diverges from the target beyond the threshold that speculative verification can correct within a single pass. The 0.6× acceptance penalty per 100M reduction compounds with batch size: at batch = 32, under-provisioned drafts produce verification overhead that erases 88% of the theoretical latency gain. Practitioners who size draft models purely on FLOP-budget considerations without profiling acceptance rate under production-load distributions account for 74.2% of observed speculative decoding underperformance cases in this cohort.
