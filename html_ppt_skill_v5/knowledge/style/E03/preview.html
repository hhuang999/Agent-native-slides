The RAPID-WATCH array at 26.5°N recorded a mean AMOC strength of 17.2 Sv for 2004–2008, declining to 15.6 Sv by 2019–2023 — a net reduction of 1.6 Sv over the continuous monitoring period. Extended proxy reconstructions using sediment core benthic δ13C records push the comparison back to 1950, indicating a broader 15 percent decline from the mid-century baseline of approximately 18.3 Sv.

The post-2012 acceleration phase is notable. Greenland Ice Sheet meltwater discharge increased by 38 percent between 2012 and 2022, introducing a freshwater anomaly of roughly 1,050 Gt·yr⁻¹ into the North Atlantic subpolar gyre. Freshwater suppresses deep convection by reducing surface density — the correlation between this flux and observed AMOC weakness is 0.84 (p < 0.001), the strongest such signal in the instrumental period.

High-freshwater years (discharge >1,100 Gt) show AMOC anomalies of −0.8 to −1.2 Sv in the following 18–24 months, consistent with Labrador Sea convection suppression.
Low-freshwater periods (2000–2009) correspond to relative AMOC stability — mean transport deviation within ±0.3 Sv of the 2004–2024 mean.
