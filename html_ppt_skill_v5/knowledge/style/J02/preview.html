Finding 01 — Detection Velocity

Routes Monitored by Real-Time Multi-Modal Tracking Detected Disruption Events 6.2 Days Earlier on Average
Than Routes Relying on Supplier Self-Reporting, Enabling Rerouting Before Backlog Formation in 78 Percent of Cases.

Routes Analysed 1,847 active routes Covered by Multi-Modal Monitoring ↑ 68% of network

Routes instrumented with GLAP's multi-modal telemetry layer — integrating AIS vessel tracking, air cargo manifest feeds, and road sensor networks — consistently surfaced disruption signals days before supplier-generated status updates. In Q3 2024, the median self-reporting lag was 8.7 days from event onset; multi-modal detection achieved a median of 2.5 days. The 6.2-day advantage proved decisive: logistics teams that received early alerts rerouted shipments before origin buffers were exhausted in 78% of cases, compared to only 47% on self-reported routes. Backlog clearing time on successfully rerouted lanes averaged 4.1 days versus 22.8 days for reactive interventions — a 5.6× efficiency gain across all monitored disruption categories.
